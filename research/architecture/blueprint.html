01

The argument

RNA 3D prediction sits near TM-score 0.55 — the threshold for recovering roughly the right fold — while protein prediction routinely exceeds 0.9. The most informative result in the recent literature says the gap is not a scale problem.

What ERNIE-RNA actually does

One additive bias on attention logits

Aij = QiKjᵀ/√d + Bpair(i,j)

Bpair is set by hand: 3 for C–G, 2 for A–U, 0.8 for G–U, 0 on the diagonal. That is the whole advantage — and it nearly doubles cross-family F₁ against a model 7.5× its size, because a physical prior holds across families where learned statistics do not.

Thesis

If a crude three-valued prior outperforms 7.5× the parameters, then the marginal return on better physics exceeds the marginal return on more scale. PHAROS replaces that hand-set prior with a learned, ion-aware, motif-aware one.

02

Measured evidence

Three findings measured for this report on 180 BGSU non-redundant representative structures at ≤3.0 Å. Each one forces a specific architectural decision.

Finding 1 — RNA contact maps scale O(L), not O(L²)

117 chains · contact = any heavy-atom pair ≤ 8 Å, |i−j| ≥ 4

Forces

A dense L×L pair track would need 309 GB of activations at L=2048 and would spend over 99% of its compute on empty space. Sparsity is the ground truth, not an approximation. But how you exploit it turns out to matter just as much — a flat top-K selection fails on exactly the long chains where it is needed. That measurement, and the design it forced, are in §07.

Finding 2 — Ion coordination and rigidity are one phenomenon

24,623 nucleotides · 15 X-ray structures containing Mg²⁺ · B-factor z-normalised within each structure · cryo-EM excluded (ADPs not comparable) and a ≥30-RNA-residue guard applied

Forces

Ion effects and motif rigidity are not independent features. Predicting Mg²⁺ sites yields a rigidity map nearly for free and vice versa, so they share one physics expert rather than two disconnected heads.

The obvious confound, tested. Mg²⁺ proximity and dense packing both mark folded cores, so the gradient might merely restate that cores are ordered. Controlling for density reduces the association only from +0.420 to +0.372 (partial correlation) — the signal is largely independent of packing. A within-structure estimate gives +1.514 σ, positive in 4/4 structures holding both groups, bootstrap 95% CI [+1.256, +1.661]. The selection objection is closed too: the gradient uses a ≥30-residue guard, and small RNAs are where the inner-sphere Mg²⁺ fraction is lowest (0.296 vs 0.511), so the exclusion could have inflated it. Recomputing at guards 20/15/10/5 and with no guard at all moves the span only from 1.760 to 1.757 σ and it stays monotonic throughout — a variance control, not a bias. Remaining caveats: B-factor absorbs resolution and refinement choices; the closest bin holds 319 nucleotides; the within-structure estimate rests on 4 structures. Association, not demonstrated causation.

Cation inventory across 179 parsed structures

83% of direct Mg²⁺ coordination is to the non-bridging phosphate oxygens OP1/OP2; the next class is the major-groove guanine site G(O6)/G(N7). Coordination is mostly water-mediated: 0.77 inner-sphere vs 7.00 outer-sphere contacts per ion.

06

What could sink it

R1

Block-detection recall. Superseded but not eliminated — the flat design was measured at ~20% recall and replaced (§07). The hierarchical track has a 100% recall ceiling by construction, but whether a trained model attains it is unproven. Recall must be reported per length bin and per level.

R2

Ionic metadata — audited, partially confirmed (§08). ~36% of structures yield a recoverable ionic condition. Worse, recorded Mg²⁺ spans only 5–15 mM because the PDB is survivorship-biased. The learned ionic response must come from RMDB titration series, not the PDB. The closed-form physics term and the Mg²⁺ site head are unaffected.

R3

Crystallographic ions are not the solution ensemble. Resolved Mg²⁺ are site-bound only; the diffuse Manning atmosphere that neutralises ~80% of backbone charge (A-form RNA, θ = 0.804) is invisible to crystallography. The explicit head learns site binding, not the atmosphere.

R4

B-factor is a noisy rigidity proxy, absorbing resolution, occupancy and refinement choices. Excluding non-comparable cryo-EM ADPs and applying a ≥30-RNA-residue guard left 31 of 137 parsed structures usable for the density/GNRA analyses, and only 15 of those contain Mg²⁺ for the gradient itself.

R5

The chemical-probing gap. Ribonanza's 2.1M DMS/SHAPE-probed sequences — ~315× more supervised examples than the 6,661 with 3D — are not in the catalogue. Highest-priority acquisition.

G2

99% of 3D supervision is RNA in complex (§14). The model predicts single chains but learns folds stabilised by unseen partners. Not fixable by tuning. Performance must be reported split by isolated vs complexed.

G3

Residue-weighted statistics are 93% ribosomal (§14). The length-stratified tables show the sparse budget is safe at both ends, but the headline figures must be labelled as ribosome measurements.

G7

1.04% of RNA polymer residues fall outside vocab-5 (§14) — DNA hybrids, inosine, UNK. "Any RNA" is not supportable without widening the structural vocabulary or stating the N-mapping.

G6

Router behaviour at recycle 0 is undefined (§14). Belec got an explicit first-pass rule; the router did not.

R7

Ensemble evaluation is barely defined (§13). No settled metric exists for scoring a predicted RNA ensemble against experiment; RNAnneal compares against just 16 experimentally-resolved riboswitch conformations. Single-state TM-score on blind sets stays the headline; ensemble output is reported descriptively until a protocol is agreed in advance.

R8

The dynamics output is equilibrium-only (§13). It models breathing about a fold, not the folding pathway. Do not describe it as predicting how RNA folds.

R6

Evaluation honesty. Any improvement claim must be made on blind sets (CASP15/16, RNA-Puzzles), never on rRNA-saturated random splits. Sequence-identity deduplication is mandatory.

07

What validation changed

The efficiency claim was the load-bearing one, so it was tested rather than assumed. It did not survive. This section records the failure and the design it forced — the most useful result of the session.

The flat top-K design collapses on long chains

Recall of true 3D contacts in the top K = 32L, sequence-only proposer

Discarded

The headline mean of 0.795 is almost entirely an artefact of short chains, where K = 32L already covers 59% of the map — a random scorer reaches 0.746. Where sparsity actually matters, the flat design recovers one contact in five. A banded fallback fails too: median contact separation is 78 nt on long chains, and even a ±512 band misses 19%.

BUGS

Two silent defects found while building it, both of which would have degraded a trained model rather than crashing. The L3 expansion paired block rows with columns element-wise, materialising only each block's diagonal — 4 pairs per 4×4 block instead of 16. And the L2 budget, expressed as a fraction of all pairs, was clamped by masking to a quarter of target. Both pinned effective c at 5.0 while the code appeared to work. Budgets are now stated in the same units as the measurement (K = c·L).

Testing found a defect the benchmark could not

the speed benchmark ran under torch.no_grad(), so this was invisible to it

BUG

The L1 and L2 block scorers received no gradient at all — their parameters were dead. The cause is structural, not a slip: the scores feed only topk, which is non-differentiable. The selected indices gather pair features, but the scores themselves never reach the loss. As specified, the block detectors could never have been trained and would have stayed at random initialisation — risk R1, shown to be structural rather than hypothetical.

Two fixes, both implemented. Score gating multiplies each pair feature by σ(sb2 + sb1) so gradient from the contact loss reaches the selectors — measured cost none (L=4096 still 0.36 s, 0.959% of dense, identical pair counts). And an auxiliary block-occupancy loss, the primary signal, supervises the selectors directly with binary labels we already have from the occupancy measurement:

Lblock = BCE(sb1, occupiedb1) + BCE(sb2, occupiedb2)

Implemented and tested in cycle 2 — until then only a hook existed (the scores were exposed; the loss was never written). block_occupancy_loss() now computes recall-weighted BCE with pos_weight = neg/pos. Gradient reaches all 6 selector parameters through this loss alone, and 60 Adam steps drive the loss 3.0278 → 0.0000 with block recall 0.000 → 1.000 at both levels. That is a single-example overfit on a synthetic stem-plus-cluster pattern: it demonstrates the mechanism works, not that it generalizes across real RNA.

Changes R1

Blocks are no longer something we hope a trained model finds — they are a directly supervised prediction target with labels already in hand, and block recall is measurable per level during training rather than only at the end. Imbalance is severe and known (1.34% positive), so the term needs recall-weighting: a missed block is unrecoverable, a spurious one only costs compute.

OPEN

What this does not prove. The measurements show the information is present and the cost affordable. They do not show that a trained model will find the occupied blocks — that needs training. The nearest external evidence is ERNIE-RNA's zero-shot Top-L/1 contact precision of 0.68 from attention alone, which suggests learned representations rank contacts far better than the sequence-only heuristic tested here.

Survivorship bias

Mg²⁺ spans only 5–15 mM, and this is not a sampling accident. Nobody deposits a structure of unfolded RNA. Why this must be learned, not tabulated

Gaussian NLL per step over all 103,965 steps — lower is better

Rules out the table

Sequence and structural context are near-equal, complementary contributors. Held-out on the common subset: sequence +1.7847, structure-only +1.7409, and combining them adds a further +1.0336 beyond sequence. A dinucleotide table is sequence-only, so it leaves ~1.03 nats unused and covers only 86.7% of steps — the structurally unusual ones, which matter most, get no entry at all.

Corrected in cycle 2 (REV-2). An earlier version of this page claimed structure contributes more than sequence (+3.028 vs +2.144). That was an artefact: each model was fitted in-sample and scored on its own covered subset (103,965 / 90,098 / 78,076 steps), and finer partitioning lowers in-sample NLL mechanically. Rescored fairly, the gain shrinks 2.9× and the order inverts. The learned-encoder decision stands; the superlative does not.

The stiffness encoder

replaces the table; F becomes the force-constant field for Erigid

Lstiff = Σi [ ½(xi − μi)ᵀ Fi (xi − μi) − ½ log det Fi ]

Per step the encoder emits a mean μ ∈ ℝ⁶ and a 6×6 positive-definite precision F, Cholesky-parameterised so positive-definiteness is structural rather than penalised. The log-det term is load-bearing: without it the degenerate optimum is F → 0 — declare everything floppy and pay nothing. With it, confidence has to be earned.

GATE

Acceptance criterion. Must reach below 15.3424 nats/step (fair held-out M2) on held-out structures. Above 16.376 (held-out sequence-only) it is worse than a lookup table and the component should be cut.

Making it trainable

NucleicBERT used 192 A100s densely. That is the bar to get under.

Muon — the largest optimiser win available

orthogonalised gradient momentum via Newton–Schulz

Compute efficiency vs AdamW~2×
FLOPs to match AdamW quality~52%
Memory saving~33%

Moonlight (3B/16B MoE, 5.7T tokens) and Kimi K2 — so it is proven on exactly the architecture class used here. Applies to 2D matrices (expert FFNs, attention projections); embeddings and norms stay on AdamW.

CAVEAT

Fewer optimiser steps, but each step costs more — Newton–Schulz is cubic. The net win favours large hidden dimensions and must be benchmarked on our actual shapes rather than assumed.

The physics-guided objective

L = LMLM + L2D + Lcontact + Ldist + λ₁Lstiff + λ₂LMg + λ₃Lelec + λ₄Lviolation + λ₅Lflex

The precedent that physics terms help rather than hurt is OpenMM-Loss, which puts molecular-dynamics potential energy into AlphaFold2 as a differentiable loss and reports comparable accuracy with lower potential energy and better MolProbity scores. For Lviolation the current best RNA parameter sets are AMBER χOL3 and HB-CUFIX (2025), the latter outperforming χOL3 and ROC against NMR and SAXS. Every label above came from data already in hand — no new acquisition.

Hierarchical Reasoning Models — adopt the loop, not the hierarchy

Adopted

Deep supervision at every segment (we previously computed loss only after the final recycle); one-step gradient — detach hidden states between segments, which decouples cycle count from activation memory so effective depth becomes free in memory terms; and train with more cycles than inference uses (train 8–16, serve 3), since the training-time loop carries the gain. ACT halting deferred — RNA motivates it better than fixed puzzle grids, since a helix needs one pass and a four-way junction many.

NOT ADOPTED

And a caveat that must travel with the citation: the same independent ARC Prize analysis that credits the outer loop also finds cross-task transfer is limited and that most of HRM's ARC-AGI performance comes from memorising solutions to the evaluation tasks. HRM is a zero-pretraining test-time-training approach. That weakens it as evidence for a model that must generalize to unseen RNA — especially given §14. The loop stands on its measured merits; the parameter-count argument should not lean on HRM.

HRM's two-timescale H/L split — measured at ~5pp and possibly noise. PHAROS already has a hierarchy (coarse blocks → fine pairs) justified by measured block occupancy rather than by analogy to brain oscillations. The architectural novelty in HRM was oversold; the loop is what works.

Dynamics: what the stiffness field licenses

The brief asked for structure prediction "or maybe more to it, maybe dynamics of the RNA, maybe how it will fold". §09 produced a force-constant field. This is what it supports — and, just as deliberately, what it does not.

One structure is the wrong output for much of RNA

why an ensemble, not a coordinate

Riboswitches are defined by holding two folds — apo and holo. Predicting one predicts half the biology.

The field has moved off the minimum-free-energy structure: functional RNAs are heterogeneous ensembles in which the most stable state may be a minor subpopulation.

CASP16 now scores alternative conformations as its own track. On how many states: gRNAde reports a consistent 3–5% gain, best at 3; RNAnneal uses 10. PHAROS emits K = 3.

The ensemble is nearly free once F exists

assemble F → diagonalise → normal modes · no sampling, no MD

OutputSupervision
per-step 6×6 stiffnessmeasured F, 76 contexts
per-nt fluctuation amplitudeB-factors / RMSF
per-residue disorder46,448 RNA unobserved-residue records
K = 3 states + weightsCASP16 alt-conformations
Cheapest addition in the design

A residue in _pdbx_unobs_or_zero_occ_residues is one too mobile or disordered to model — a direct per-residue flexibility label. It sits in every deposited structure, is used by no RNA structure predictor, and we already extracted 46,448 RNA ones (the raw category total of 143,874 is 67.5% protein) at zero acquisition cost.

LIMIT

This is an equilibrium fluctuation model. It describes breathing around a fold. It is not a folding-pathway predictor and must not be presented as one. Cotranscriptional folding is kinetic — structure evolves as the chain elongates and the system rarely reaches equilibrium, so cotranscriptional products routinely deviate from thermodynamic predictions. Reconstructing those pathways needs time-resolved probing data (the R2D2 approach), which is in neither our catalogue nor Ribonanza. Calling a harmonic ensemble "predicts how RNA folds" would be exactly the overclaim the coevolution retraction in §02 was about. The supportable claim: predicts the fold, its local rigidity, its disorder, and the amplitude of motion about it, under stated ionic conditions.

The design claims contact maps, structure and other properties for RNA in general. Tested against the 180 sampled structures rather than asserted. Four limits are real; three were undocumented before this audit.

G2 — 99% of the evidence is RNA in complex

The architecture takes one sequence and predicts its fold — but essentially all 3D supervision comes from RNA stabilised by partners the model never sees. Defect #24 — partial propagation

The first pass at this correction updated four of the eleven affected numbers and stopped, leaving three tables mixing the 180 and 162 denominators inside one row-set; and an unguarded global replace overwrote the published column of this very table, making it read "99.70 → 99.70". Third occurrence of that failure mode. Defect #25 — half the format was never parsed

mmCIF serialises a category two ways: a loop_ form, and a key-value form (_entity_poly.tag  value) used when the category has exactly one row. The resolver handled only the first, so 16 of 180 structures silently parsed to zero RNA. They are not a random sixteen — one polymer entity means a small isolated RNA, precisely the population G2 makes a claim about — and their absence inflated "RNA residues in complexes" from 98.95% to a spurious 99.70%. Cycle 6 reported that inflation as a finding: "G2 strengthens". A defect introduced while auditing a definition produced a false result which the audit then published, and it surfaced only because the next cycle asked why 18 entries in a non-redundant RNA list appeared to contain no RNA.

Defect #26 — the same bug, in the other parser

With #25 fixed in the RNA resolver, the next question is whether any other parser shares it. loops() — used by the geometry, stiffness and ionic scripts — kept only the tag from each _category.tag line and threw away a value on the same line, so a key-value category yielded zero rows, silently, exactly as before. Measured: 9 rows across 9 structures. Impact on published numbers: +1 base-pair step (103,964 → 103,965), +1 RNA disorder record (46,447 → 46,448), one more structure with annotations (155 → 156). The 115.1× stiffness ratio, the A-form validation geometry and the 76 stiffness contexts are unchanged. Negligible in 180 files — but the corpus is not 180 files, and the identical bug in _entity_poly moved a headline finding by 0.75 points. _exptl and _exptl_crystal_grow are key-value in every file carrying them and are read by a separate, key-value-aware parser — checked, not assumed.

Defect #27 — the guard was looser than the effects it guards

#26 was nearly missed because the regression guard reported OK for annotated base-pair steps = 103,965 against an expected 103,964: every claim defaulted to a 0.5% relative tolerance, and one part in 103,964 is far inside it. Across all 54 numeric claims, 45 had a tolerance wider than the precision they quote. The worst: G7 frac of RNA residues, checked as 0.01047 ± 0.01 — it would have accepted anything from 0.0005 to 0.0205, a vacuous guard on the single figure this audit corrected by 8.5×. The default is now derived from how the value is written: an integer must match exactly, a decimal to half a unit in its last quoted place. All 54 still pass — the claims were accurate to their stated digits; the guard was not proving it. Verified adversarially: injecting the retracted cycle-6 value (0.9970) and a +1 count drift both now fail the build, and both passed before.

The conflict

The real lever

The token cut is a 12.9× saving against FP8's 1.6× — and unlike a precision lever it costs no accuracy, because the dropped tokens are redundant rather than informative. Cutting 323B → 25B gives 410 tok/param, still deliberately over-trained for inference efficiency, without entering the degradation corner.

Where the over-provisioned bits actually go

the "more attributes, fewer bits" idea — corrected

Measured: a fully attributed nucleotide carries 58.9 bits; a 512-dim bf16 token holds 8,192 — 139× over-provisioned. So d_model is compute space, not storage.

The correct form of the idea: attributes enter as input features through one projection. They do not justify a wider d_model — training FLOPs go as 6·N_active and widening d is quadratic in the FFN. Attributes are nearly free; width is not.

CARE

Only 26.0 of the 58.9 bits are available at inference for an arbitrary sequence (identity, position, local GC, recycled pairing state, ionic condition, in-complex flag). The rest — B-factor, Mg distance, Saenger class, disorder — are supervision targets, not inputs. Feeding them in would leak the answer.

"4.4× smaller" collapses to 1.65×

By active parameters, 61M vs 269M is 4.4×. By effective compute (active × loops) it is 488M vs 807M — only 1.65×. Eight loops against three consume most of the saving.

Corrected

The lever chain falls from a claimed 24× to 5.6×, because right-sizing is 1.65× rather than 4.43× once loops are counted. At the decided 25B budget PHAROS-Small costs 78 A100-hours in bf16, or 12 on H100 fp8 — two independently written cost paths agree on both. The token cut (12.9×) is therefore the largest single lever in the design — larger than right-sizing and larger than any precision choice.
