\begin{abstract}
\noindent
RNA three-dimensional structure prediction has not had its AlphaFold2 moment.
The best current methods reach a mean TM-score of approximately $0.55$ on
non-redundant benchmarks, which is the threshold for recovering roughly the
correct fold, against routine scores above $0.9$ for proteins. This report
argues that the bottleneck is not model scale but \emph{inductive bias}, and
specifies an architecture, \textbf{PHAROS}, built around that claim.

Three empirical findings drive the design. First, ERNIE-RNA (86M parameters)
outperforms RiNALMo (650M) and UNI-RNA (400M) on secondary-structure prediction,
and nearly doubles cross-family generalisation relative to RiNALMo ($F_1 = 0.646$
versus $0.355$), on the strength of a single hand-set, three-valued base-pairing
prior added to its attention logits. Second, we measure on 117 non-redundant RNA
chains that contacts per nucleotide \emph{saturates} at $4.4$--$4.9$ while
contact-map density falls from $6.5\%$ at $L \approx 56$ to $0.353\%$ at
$L \approx 2900$: RNA contact maps scale as $O(L)$, not $O(L^2)$, so a dense
pair representation spends over $99\%$ of its compute on empty space and would
require $309$~GB of activations at $L=2048$. Third, we measure a monotonic
$1.76\sigma$ gradient in normalised crystallographic B-factor as a function of
distance to the nearest $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ ion across $24{,}623$ nucleotides,
establishing that ion coordination and local rigidity are not independent
features but two readouts of one phenomenon.

PHAROS accordingly (i) accepts \textbf{ionic conditions as a model input},
which no existing RNA structure predictor does, propagating them to attention
logits through a Manning-renormalised screened-Coulomb bias---though an audit
reported here shows the \emph{learned} part of that claim cannot be trained on
the PDB, whose recorded Mg\textsuperscript{2+} spans only 5--15 mM because
nobody deposits unfolded RNA; (ii) replaces the
dense pair track with a \textbf{hierarchical coarse-to-fine pair track} that
selects contact-bearing \emph{blocks} rather than pairs, at $2.2\%$ of dense
cost---adopted after we measured a flat top-$K$ design recovering only
\emph{one contact in five} on long chains, and measured block occupancy falling
to $1.34\%$ as chains lengthen; (iii) routes a \textbf{sparse
mixture-of-experts} on local structural regime rather than sequence context,
supported by our negative result that GNRA $k$-mer context predicts rigidity at
only $0.073\sigma$; and (iv) supplies motif geometry through a \textbf{frozen
retrieval bank} of 667 RNA 3D Motif Atlas classes rather than expecting the
model to learn it from 6{,}661 structurally-resolved sequences. We also identify
two supervision channels---$\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ sites and B-factor
rigidity---that are already present in every mmCIF file the field downloads and
are currently used by no structure predictor.

This is a design specification. No model has been trained. All quantitative
claims are either marked \meas{} and reproducible from the scripts in this
repository, or cited to the literature.
\end{abstract}

A TM-score near $0.5$ means the topology is approximately right and little else.
The CASP16 signal is equally instructive: leading RNA groups won by integrating

network. Physics-plus-machine-learning hybrids are currently ahead in RNA,
which is the premise this architecture is built on.

\subsection{Why RNA lags}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item \textbf{Data scarcity.} Our catalogue records $6{,}661$ unique sequences
with experimental 3D structure against $1.37$ billion sequences, a coverage of
$0.0005\%$. Proteins had roughly $220{,}000$ structures at the AlphaFold2
moment.
\item \textbf{Redundancy within that data.} $15{,}441$ RNA3DB chains collapse to
$3{,}157$ unique sequences; 5S rRNA alone accounts for $5{,}976$ BGSU
equivalence classes.
\item \textbf{Family coverage.} Only $100$ of $4{,}227$ Rfam families ($2.4\%$)
have any 3D representative.
\item \textbf{Conformational plasticity.} Riboswitches are \emph{defined} by
adopting multiple functional folds; single-structure prediction is the wrong
target for a large fraction of functional RNA.
\item \textbf{Ion dependence.} RNA is a polyanion. Its fold is not defined
without specifying the ionic environment, yet no mainstream predictor accepts
ionic conditions as an input.
\item \textbf{Target noise.} Median resolution in our catalogue is $3.10$~\AA{},
with only $13\%$ of chains at $2.5$~\AA{} or better.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The central argument: bias beats scale}

Table~\ref{tab:ernie} contains the most important result in the recent RNA
language-model literature.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{ERNIE-RNA versus larger dense models. A 7.5$\times$ parameter deficit
is overturned by one hand-set prior.}
\label{tab:ernie}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Params} & \textbf{bpRNA-1m $F_1$} & \textbf{bpRNA-new $F_1$} \\
\midrule
ERNIE-RNA (attention map) & 86M  & $\mathbf{0.873}$ & --- \\
ERNIE-RNA (embedding)     & 86M  & $0.862$ & $\mathbf{0.646}$ \\
RiNALMo                   & 650M & $0.850$ & $0.355$ \\
UNI-RNA                   & 400M & $0.821$ & --- \\
RNA-FM                    & 100M & $0.808$ & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

ERNIE-RNA's entire architectural advantage is an additive bias on attention
logits. In its first layer,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Attention}(Q,K) = \frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}} + B_{\text{pair}}(i,j),
\qquad
\mathrm{Attention}_{l}(Q,K) = \frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}} + \mathrm{Attention}_{l-1}(Q,K),
\end{equation}
with $B_{\text{pair}}$ set by hand to $3$ for C--G, $2$ for A--U, a tunable
$\alpha \approx 0.8$ for G--U, and $0$ on the diagonal.

The cross-family result is the decisive one: $0.646$ versus $0.355$. A fixed
physical prior nearly doubles generalisation to unseen families, because the
prior is true regardless of family whereas learned family statistics are not.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Thesis.} If a crude three-valued prior buys more than $7\times$ the
parameters, the marginal return on \emph{better physics} exceeds the marginal
return on \emph{more scale}. PHAROS replaces that hand-set prior with a learned,
ion-aware, motif-aware, coevolution-aware one.
\end{quote}

Its limitations define our openings. The prior is static, so a G--U pair inside
a kink-turn is treated identically to one in a free helix. It is
nearest-neighbour-blind, whereas real pairing energy is stacking-dependent. It
encodes only canonical pairing, while non-canonical pairs define RNA 3D motifs
and carry most tertiary information. And it ignores ions entirely.

\section{Empirical analysis}

All results in this section were measured for this report on 180 BGSU
non-redundant representative structures at $\leq 3.0$~\AA{}, downloaded and
analysed by scripts in \texttt{.../sampling/}. Raw outputs are in
\texttt{data/samples/analysis/}.

\subsection{RNA contact maps scale linearly, not quadratically}

Contacts are defined as nucleotide pairs with any heavy-atom pair within
$8.0$~\AA{} and sequence separation $|i-j| \geq 4$.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Contact density by chain length across 117 chains. \meas}
\label{tab:sparsity}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Chain length} & \textbf{$n$} & \textbf{median $L$} & \textbf{contacts/nt} & \textbf{map density} \\
\midrule
32--100    & 38 & 56   & $1.784$ & $6.523\%$ \\
100--200   & 6  & 124  & $2.851$ & $4.043\%$ \\
200--500   & 3  & 393  & $4.216$ & $2.151\%$ \\
500--1500  & 9  & 1038 & $4.532$ & $0.974\%$ \\
$\geq 1500$ & 61 & 2901 & $4.854$ & $\mathbf{0.353\%}$ \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textbf{Overall}} & & median $4.397$, p95 $5.422$, max $5.497$ & median $0.389\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Contacts per nucleotide saturates at $4.4$--$4.9$ and is bounded by RNA
coordination geometry, independent of length; density falls approximately as
$1/L$. The number of contacts therefore grows \emph{linearly} in $L$.

The consequence for architecture is immediate. A dense pair representation of
dimension $c=128$ requires, for 48 blocks holding roughly six intermediate
tensors each and without gradient checkpointing:
\[
L=512 \rightarrow 19.3~\text{GB}, \qquad
L=1024 \rightarrow 77.3~\text{GB}, \qquad
L=2048 \rightarrow 309.2~\text{GB}.
\]
A dense pair track is not merely wasteful at RNA lengths; it is infeasible.
How that sparsity is exploited turns out to matter as much as exploiting it at
all: a flat top-$K$ selection fails on long chains, and contacts must be
selected as \emph{blocks}. Section~\ref{sec:hpt} reports both measurements.

\subsection{Ion coordination and local rigidity are one phenomenon}

We z-normalise crystallographic B-factors within each structure so that
refinement protocols are comparable. Lower $z_B$ indicates greater rigidity.

\paragraph{Sample definition.} Of the 180 sampled structures, 63 are X-ray and
117 cryo-EM; cryo-EM atomic displacement parameters are not comparable and are
excluded. A further guard requires at least 30 RNA residues with a resolved
C1$'$, removing 32 small X-ray entries (2--28 residues). That leaves \textbf{31
X-ray structures} for the packing-density and sequence-context analyses, and the
\textbf{15 of those containing $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$} for the ion-distance analysis
below.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Normalised B-factor versus distance to the nearest $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$,
over $24{,}623$ nucleotides in 15 X-ray structures. \meas}
\label{tab:mgrigid}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Distance to nearest $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$} & \textbf{$n$ nucleotides} & \textbf{mean $z_B$} \\
\midrule
$0$--$4$~\AA{}    & 319   & $\mathbf{-0.883}$ \\
$4$--$6$~\AA{}    & 2{,}441 & $-0.673$ \\
$6$--$8$~\AA{}    & 7{,}590 & $-0.415$ \\
$8$--$12$~\AA{}   & 8{,}115 & $+0.004$ \\
$12$--$20$~\AA{}  & 2{,}984 & $+0.756$ \\
$> 20$~\AA{}      & 3{,}174 & $\mathbf{+0.877}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The gradient is monotonic and spans $1.76$ standard deviations. Local packing
density independently correlates with rigidity at Pearson $r = -0.294$.

\paragraph{The obvious confound, tested.} $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ proximity and dense
packing both mark folded cores, so the gradient might merely restate that cores
are ordered. An independent re-derivation tested this directly:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Pearson$(z_B,\ d_{\mathrm{Mg}})$ & $+0.4197$ \\
Spearman$(z_B,\ d_{\mathrm{Mg}})$ & $+0.5082$ \\
Pearson$(d_{\mathrm{Mg}},\ \text{density})$ --- the confound & $-0.2513$ \\
\textbf{Partial} corr$(z_B,\ d_{\mathrm{Mg}} \mid \text{density})$ & $\mathbf{+0.3722}$ \\
Partial corr$(z_B,\ \text{density} \mid d_{\mathrm{Mg}})$ & $-0.2273$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Controlling for packing density reduces the $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ association only
from $0.420$ to $0.372$, so the signal is largely \emph{independent} of packing.

\paragraph{The selection objection, also tested.} The gradient applies a
$\geq30$-RNA-residue guard, and small RNAs are exactly where the inner-sphere
$\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ fraction is lowest ($0.296$ versus $0.511$), so the exclusion
could have inflated the result. Sweeping the guard shows it does not:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Guard} & \textbf{Structures} & \textbf{Nucleotides} & \textbf{Span ($\sigma$)} & \textbf{Monotonic} \\
\midrule
$\geq 30$ & 15 & 24{,}623 & $\mathbf{1.760}$ & yes \\
$\geq 20$ & 16 & 24{,}646 & $1.757$ & yes \\
$\geq 10$ & 17 & 24{,}660 & $1.757$ & yes \\
none      & 19 & 24{,}666 & $\mathbf{1.757}$ & yes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent The span moves by $0.003\sigma$ across the entire sweep and stays
monotonic throughout: a variance control, not a bias.
A within-structure estimate---comparing nucleotides within 4~\AA{} against those
beyond 12~\AA{} inside the same structure, which removes all between-structure
heterogeneity---gives $+1.514\sigma$, positive in $4/4$ structures that contain
both groups, bootstrap 95\% CI $[+1.256, +1.661]$. The gradient is not an
artefact of pooling, though $n=4$ makes this supportive rather than decisive.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Architectural consequence.} Ion effects and motif rigidity, which the
project brief treats as two separate physical ingredients, are strongly coupled.
A model that predicts $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ sites obtains a rigidity map nearly for
free and vice versa. That mutual information justifies routing them into a
\emph{shared} physics expert rather than two disconnected heads.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{The selection objection, closed.} The gradient is measured after a
$\geq 30$-RNA-residue guard, and small RNAs are precisely where the inner-sphere
Mg\textsuperscript{2+} fraction is lowest ($0.296$ against $0.511$ overall), so
the exclusion could in principle have inflated the result. Recomputing at guards
of $20$, $15$, $10$, $5$ and with the guard removed entirely moves the span only
from $1.760$ to $1.757\sigma$---a change of $0.003$---and it remains monotonic at
every threshold. Removing the guard adds 4 structures and 43 nucleotides
($+0.17\%$), because the excluded structures hold 2--28 residues each. The guard
is a variance control, not a source of bias.

\paragraph{Caveats.} B-factor also absorbs resolution, occupancy and refinement
choices. The closest distance bin contains only $319$ nucleotides, and the
within-structure estimate rests on 4 structures. The partial correlation above
substantially weakens the packing-confound objection but does not eliminate it:
this remains association rather than demonstrated causation, and is evidence for
\emph{using} the coupling as a prior rather than a proof of mechanism.

$\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ outnumbers all other cations by roughly $9\!:\!1$, so an
ion-aware model that handles a single species should handle $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$.
Some $83\%$ of direct coordination is to the two non-bridging phosphate oxygens;
the next largest class, G(O6)/G(N7), is the textbook major-groove guanine site.

Coordination is predominantly water-mediated: a mean of $0.77$ inner-sphere
against $7.00$ outer-sphere RNA contacts per $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$. Crucially the
inner-sphere fraction is itself a function of tertiary compactness: $0.511$
overall but only $0.296$ for chains shorter than $500$~nt. The inner/outer ratio
is therefore a \emph{predictable readout} of compactness, not merely an input.

\paragraph{Stratification warning.} These aggregates are ribosome-dominated and
must not be quoted flat. The five largest structures hold $39.4\%$ of all
$\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$. Per structure the median is $72.5$ ions against a mean of
$181.5$; per nucleotide the median is $0.046$, roughly one $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$
per $22$ nucleotides.

\paragraph{What crystallography cannot see.} Resolved $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ are
site-bound ions only. The diffuse Manning atmosphere, which neutralises roughly
$76\%$ of backbone charge, is invisible to crystallography. The two channels
must be modelled separately and never conflated.

\subsection{Coevolution is strong, but gated by alignment depth}
\label{sec:coev}

Coevolution is the oldest structural signal in the field: a base pair tolerates
mutation at one position only when compensated at the other, so paired columns
covary across a family alignment. We measured how much of that signal is
actually recoverable, computing sequence-reweighted, pseudocounted,
APC-corrected mutual information on the 12 downloaded Rfam seed alignments and
scoring it against each family's curated consensus secondary structure
(\texttt{\#=GC SS\_cons}), restricted to $|i-j| \geq 4$.

Splitting on $N_{\text{eff}}/L$, families at $N_{\text{eff}}/L \geq 1$ ($n=2$)
reach mean precision@$L/5$ of $0.975$ while those below ($n=10$) reach $0.609$.
\textbf{This split is weak evidence and should not be leaned on.} Tested properly:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Test} & \textbf{Result} \\
\midrule
Exact permutation test over all 66 splits & $p = 0.0455$ \\
Pearson$(N_{\text{eff}}/L,\ \text{precision})$, $n=12$ & $+0.498$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three reasons for caution: the ``deep'' arm has $n=2$; the
$N_{\text{eff}}/L = 1$ threshold was chosen \emph{post hoc}, so $p = 0.0455$ is
optimistic; and a \emph{shallow} family---THF riboswitch at
$N_{\text{eff}}/L = 0.279$---attains precision $1.000$, so depth is plainly not
necessary for a strong signal. The rank correlation of $+0.224$ is the most
honest single summary: a weak positive tendency, not a gate.

What the data \emph{does} show robustly is that precision varies enormously
between families, from $0.333$ to $1.000$. Separately, note how few families are
deep: only 2 of 12, and these are hand-curated Rfam seed alignments, among the
best-conditioned RNA alignments in existence. Automatic homology search on an
arbitrary sequence will generally do worse.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Architectural consequence.} Coevolution is not a weak feature to be
blended in at fixed weight---for some families it is very nearly sufficient on
its own, for others close to useless, and that spread ($0.333$ to $1.000$) is
itself the argument for conditional rather than fixed-weight use.
$N_{\text{eff}}/L$ is a defensible router feature: it is cheaply computable
before the model runs, and the published literature is unambiguous that shallow
RNA MSAs degrade coupling analysis---this is the stated motivation for rMSA,
RNAcmap3 and RNA-MSM. \emph{Our own $n=12$ measurement supports that gate only
weakly}, so the architectural case rests on the literature and on the observed
between-family variance, not on our depth split. Settling it would require
measuring precision against depth across hundreds of families with the threshold
fixed in advance. This also explains why RhoFold+ pairs a language model
\emph{with} MSA features rather than choosing between them: the two have
complementary failure modes.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{An implementation note worth recording.} A first version of this
measurement reported mean precision $0.039$. That was a defect, not a property
of RNA: the outer-product term in the MI normalisation broadcast to shape
$(C,q,1)$ rather than $(C,q,q)$, so only diagonal terms contributed. After
correcting it and adding standard DCA sequence reweighting at $80\%$ identity
with pseudocounts, precision rose seventeenfold to $0.670$. A plausible-looking
weak result was a silent bug.

\paragraph{Caveats.} The ground truth here is Rfam's consensus \emph{secondary}
structure, not 3D contacts, so this measures recovery of curated pairing rather
than direct tertiary contact accuracy. Twelve curated seed families is a small
and favourable sample, and the depth split has $n=2$ in one arm.
APC-corrected MI is a simplification of full DCA; pseudo-likelihood DCA would do
better. The ground truth is also \emph{incomplete}: our parser handles only the
nested WUSS brackets and ignores the alphabetic pseudoknot brackets, which
account for $53$ of $537$ pairs ($9.87\%$), so a correctly-ranked pseudoknot pair
is scored as a false positive. All of these biases push measured precision
\emph{down}, so $0.670$ is a conservative lower bound.

\subsection{A negative result that constrains the design}

We tested whether GNRA-like tetraloop sequence context alone predicts rigidity,
matching the 4-mers \{GAAA, GCAA, GAGA, GUGA, GGAA, GCGA, GUAA, GAAG\} anywhere
in a chain.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{GNRA $k$-mer context versus rigidity. \meas{} Effect size $0.073\sigma$.}
\label{tab:gnra}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Context} & \textbf{$n$ nucleotides} & \textbf{mean $z_B$} \\
\midrule
GNRA-like 4-mer & 4{,}944 & $-0.082$ \\
All other       & 95{,}768 & $-0.009$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The effect is negligible. The likely cause is methodological: a GNRA
\emph{sequence} is a GNRA \emph{tetraloop} only when it actually caps a hairpin,
and linear 4-mer matching counts mostly helical and junction occurrences.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Architectural consequence.} Sequence context alone does not identify a
motif. Motif identity requires the interaction graph. The motif machinery must
therefore be conditioned on the pair representation after secondary structure is
available, never on raw $k$-mers. This rules out the naive
``learn motifs from sequence $n$-grams'' design.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Token trunk: hybrid attention with MoE}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[height=0.82\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../architecture/diagrams/rendered/02-token-trunk.png}
\caption{Period-8 mixer interleaving, repeated four times for 32 blocks.}
\end{figure}

The trunk uses \textbf{16 blocks at $d=512$} with the repeating pattern
\[
[\,\mathrm{GDN},\ \mathrm{GDN},\ \mathrm{SWA},\ \mathrm{GDN},\ \mathrm{GDN},\ \mathrm{SWA},\ \mathrm{GDN},\ \mathrm{FULL}\,],
\]
where GDN is Gated DeltaNet linear attention ($O(L)$, 10 of 16 blocks), SWA is
sliding-window softmax attention with window 128 ($O(Lw)$, 4 blocks), and FULL
is full attention with physics bias ($O(L^2)$, only 2 blocks). Depth does not
come from block count: 16 blocks $\times$ 8 refinement loops give
\textbf{128 effective layers} (Sections~\ref{sec:loop} and~\ref{sec:size}).

The justification is both architectural and biological. Jamba interleaves
Mamba, attention and MoE at a $7\!:\!1$ ratio and reaches 256K context with a
$4$~GB KV cache; Gated DeltaNet outperforms Mamba2 and DeltaNet on in-context
retrieval and length extrapolation; Oryx varies the mixer type \emph{within} a
sequence. RNA has exactly the structure this exploits: long stretches of regular
A-form helix that are locally periodic and low-information, punctuated by rare
junctions, pseudoknots and kissing loops that require global mixing. At
$L=4096$, a dense 16-block trunk would perform $268$M pair evaluations; PHAROS
performs $34$M in its two full-attention blocks---the same $8\times$ reduction on
the quadratic term, preserved under right-sizing. Counting the sliding-window
blocks as well, the honest overall ratio is $\sim$$7.5\times$.

\subsubsection{Physics-biased attention}

In SWA and FULL blocks the attention logits carry four additive terms:
\begin{equation}
A_{ij} = \frac{Q_i K_j^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}
       + B_{\mathrm{wc}}(i,j)
       + B_{\mathrm{elec}}(i,j;\,c_{\text{ion}})
       + B_{\mathrm{motif}}(i,j)
       + A^{(l-1)}_{ij}.
\end{equation}

$B_{\mathrm{wc}}$ generalises ERNIE-RNA's fixed prior to a learned pair-type
embedding modulated by stacking context, since real pairing energy is
nearest-neighbour dependent. It is initialised at the ERNIE-RNA values so
training begins from a known-good prior. The term $A^{(l-1)}$ retains
ERNIE-RNA's attention accumulation.

$B_{\mathrm{elec}}$ is the novel term. With Bjerrum length
$\ell_B = e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r k_B T) \approx 7.1$~\AA{} in water
at $298$~K, Manning parameter $\xi = \ell_B/b$ for axial charge spacing $b$,
condensed fraction and renormalised charge
\begin{equation}
\theta = 1 - \frac{1}{z\xi}, \qquad q_{\text{eff}} = -(1-\theta),
\end{equation}
and Debye screening parameter
$\kappa = \sqrt{2 N_A e^2 I / (\varepsilon\varepsilon_0 k_B T)}$ from ionic
strength $I$, the bias is
\begin{equation}
B_{\mathrm{elec}}(i,j) = -\lambda\, q_{\text{eff}}^{2}\, \ell_B\,
\frac{\exp(-\kappa d_{ij})}{d_{ij}}.
\label{eq:belec}
\end{equation}
Here $b$ is the \textbf{axial} charge spacing---charges projected onto the helix
axis---not the through-backbone P--P contour distance of $\sim$5.9--7.0~\AA{},
which is a common source of error. For A-form RNA, a rise of $2.8$~\AA{} per base
pair carrying two phosphate charges gives $b = 1.40$~\AA{}, hence $\xi = 5.11$
and $\theta = 0.804$ for monovalent counterions, i.e.\ $q_{\text{eff}} = -0.196$
per phosphate. Roughly \emph{four fifths} of backbone charge is screened before
any structure-specific electrostatics is considered. The frequently quoted
$\theta \approx 0.76$ is the \textbf{B-DNA} value ($b = 1.70$~\AA{},
$\xi = 4.21$); A-form RNA has a shorter axial charge spacing and therefore
condenses more strongly. For $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ ($z=2$), $\theta = 0.902$.
Only $\lambda$ is learned; $q_{\text{eff}}$, $\kappa$ and $\ell_B$ follow in
closed form from $c_{\text{ion}}$ and temperature. Equation~\eqref{eq:belec} is
the mechanism by which salt concentration changes the prediction.

\paragraph{Resolving the circularity.} Equation~\eqref{eq:belec} requires
$d_{ij}$, which is unknown on the first pass. $B_{\mathrm{elec}}$ is therefore
disabled at recycle 0 and enabled from recycle 1 using the current predicted
distance map. Recycling is thus structural to the design rather than an
optimisation: the physics term needs a geometry estimate to evaluate, and each
recycle sharpens both.

\subsubsection{Mixture-of-experts feed-forward}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[height=0.78\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../architecture/diagrams/rendered/03-moe-routing.png}
\caption{Expert routing. Routing keys on local structural regime, not sequence.}
\end{figure}

Following DeepSeekMoE, the design uses 32 fine-grained routed experts at
$d_{\text{ff}} = 128$ plus 2 always-on shared experts, with top-4 routing giving
6 active experts of 34, in \textbf{every} block. Section~\ref{sec:cost} explains
why the original every-\emph{second}-block placement, with dense FFN in between,
was the worst trade in the design.

Shared-expert isolation matters for a specific reason here: Watson--Crick helix
formation is the highest-frequency pattern in all RNA. Placing it in an
always-on shared expert prevents all 32 routed experts from separately
relearning it, freeing them to specialise on what is rare and hard---junctions,
pseudoknots and ion pockets.

Load balancing is auxiliary-loss-free, adjusting a per-expert bias each step
rather than adding a loss term. This matters acutely here because our 3D data is
pathologically imbalanced: 5S rRNA alone accounts for $5{,}976$ BGSU clusters,
so an auxiliary balance loss would actively fight the biology.

The router input is the token hidden state concatenated with the current local
structural estimate---pairing probability, predicted rigidity, local density---and
the alignment depth $N_{\text{eff}}/L$, which gates the coevolution expert
(Section~\ref{sec:coev}). This follows directly from the GNRA negative result of
Table~\ref{tab:gnra}: routing on sequence $k$-mers does not work. It also prevents router collapse
onto rRNA, because structural regimes are far more evenly distributed across the
corpus than families are.

\paragraph{Two silent defects found while building it.} Both would have degraded
a trained model rather than crashing. The L3 expansion initially paired block

\emph{diagonal}---4 pairs per $4\times4$ block instead of 16---silently
delivering a quarter of the intended budget. Separately, the L2 selection budget
was expressed as a fraction of all pairs and was clamped by masking to a quarter
of its target. Both pinned the effective $c$ at $5.0$ while the code appeared to
work. Budgets are now stated in the same units as the measurement ($K = cL$
pairs) so any mismatch is immediately visible.

\subsubsection{Testing exposed a defect the benchmark could not}

Writing correctness tests for the reference implementation revealed that
\textbf{the L1 and L2 block scorers received no gradient at all}. Their
parameters were dead.

The cause is structural, not a coding slip: the scorers' outputs feed only
\texttt{topk}, which is non-differentiable. The selected indices then gather pair
features, but the \emph{scores themselves} never enter the loss. As originally
specified, \textcolor{risk}{\textbf{the block detectors could never be trained}}
and would have remained at random initialisation. The speed benchmark could not
catch this because it ran under \texttt{torch.no\_grad()}.

Two mechanisms fix it, both now implemented.

\textbf{Score gating.} Each retained pair feature is multiplied by
$\sigma(s_{b_2} + s_{b_1})$, the scores of the blocks that selected it, so
gradient from the contact loss reaches the selectors. Measured cost: none.
$L=4096$ remains at $0.36$~s and $0.959\%$ of dense with identical pair counts.

\textbf{An auxiliary block-occupancy loss}, which is the primary signal. The
selectors are supervised directly with binary labels---does this block contain at
least one true contact?---and those labels are exactly what the occupancy
measurement of Table~\ref{tab:blocks} computed:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{block}} = \mathrm{BCE}(s_{b_1},\, \text{occupied}_{b_1}) + \mathrm{BCE}(s_{b_2},\, \text{occupied}_{b_2}).
\end{equation}
Class imbalance is severe and known in advance ($1.34\%$ positive at $b=4$ on
long chains), so this term needs positive weighting or focal loss, and should be
\emph{recall}-weighted: a missed block is unrecoverable, a spurious one merely
costs compute.

\paragraph{Implemented and tested in cycle 2.} Until then only a \emph{hook}
existed---the scores were exposed, but the loss was never written.
\texttt{block\_occupancy\_loss()} now computes recall-weighted BCE with
$\text{pos\_weight} = \text{neg}/\text{pos}$. Gradient reaches all six selector
parameters \emph{through this loss alone}, and 60 Adam steps drive the loss
$3.0278 \rightarrow 0.0000$ with block recall $\mathbf{0.000 \rightarrow 1.000}$
at both levels. This is a single-example overfit on a synthetic
stem-plus-cluster pattern: it demonstrates that the gradient path exists and the
objective is optimisable, \emph{not} that it generalizes across real RNA.

This changes how R1 is addressed. It is no longer a matter of hoping a trained
model finds the blocks---they are a directly supervised prediction target with
labels already in hand, and block recall becomes measurable per level during
training rather than only at the end.

\paragraph{What remains unproven.} These measurements establish that the
information is present and the cost affordable. They do \emph{not} establish
that a trained model will identify occupied blocks accurately; that requires
training. The relevant external evidence is ERNIE-RNA's zero-shot Top-$L$/1
contact precision of $0.68$ from attention maps alone, which indicates that
learned representations rank contacts far better than the sequence-only
heuristic tested above. \textcolor{risk}{\textbf{Block-detection recall must be
reported per length bin and per level.}}

\subsection{Motif retrieval bank}

The RNA 3D Motif Atlas 4.12 compresses $4{,}992$ loop instances into $667$ motif
classes, $413$ internal and $254$ hairpin: a $7.5\!:\!1$ ratio indicating that
RNA tertiary structure is far more repetitive than its sequence diversity
suggests. PHAROS precomputes a frozen key--value bank over these classes, with
keys encoding the non-canonical interaction-graph signature and values encoding
internal geometry as distance-matrix eigenfeatures plus a backbone torsion
profile. The pair track cross-attends into this bank.

This is the appropriate response to data scarcity. With only $6{,}661$ unique
sequences carrying 3D structure, a model cannot reliably learn motif geometry
implicitly. The bank supplies the geometry explicitly, leaving the model to
learn only \emph{where} motifs occur---a substantially easier problem than
learning what they look like. The bank is roughly $10^3$ fixed entries shared
across a batch, so its cost is negligible. Consistent with
Table~\ref{tab:gnra}, retrieval is keyed on the interaction graph, never on
sequence $k$-mers.

\subsection{Physics module}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth,keepaspectratio]{../architecture/diagrams/rendered/05-physics-hamiltonian.png}
\caption{The hybrid implicit/explicit electrostatic treatment.}
\end{figure}

A differentiable energy is evaluated on the predicted geometry:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{total}} = E_{\text{stack}} + E_{\text{pair}}
+ E_{\text{elec}}^{\text{implicit}} + E_{\mathrm{Mg}}^{\text{explicit}}
+ E_{\text{excl}} + E_{\text{rigid}}.
\end{equation}

The implicit/explicit split follows STEM, which establishes that mean-field
theory captures monovalent KCl well but fails for $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$, where
ion--ion correlation and site-specific chelation dominate. Our own inventory
agrees on which species matters: $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ outnumbers $\mathrm{K}^{+}$
by $9\!:\!1$.

Inner/outer-sphere classification is a first-class output because STEM's central
claim is that folding dynamics are driven by \emph{exchange between} direct and
water-mediated coordination---the mode, not the presence, is decisive. Our
measurements supply both channels directly and show the inner fraction varies
with compactness ($0.511$ overall, $0.296$ below $500$~nt).

The term $E_{\text{rigid}}$ closes the loop with Table~\ref{tab:mgrigid}:
predicted $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ sites raise local rigidity, which stiffens harmonic
restraints, which constrains the decoder. That pathway is the architectural
encoding of the measured $1.76\sigma$ gradient.

\subsection{How the claim is rescoped}

The novelty splits into three components with very different evidentiary support.

\label{sec:stiffenc}

The obvious implementation is a 76-entry table keyed on dinucleotide context.
That repeats the mistake the GNRA result already disproved. We measured the
headroom by fitting Gaussians over the six step coordinates and comparing mean
negative log-likelihood per step over all 103{,}964 steps.

\textcolor{risk}{\textbf{Corrected in cycle 2 (REV-2).}} The figures above fit

subset ($103{,}964$ / $90{,}098$ / $78{,}076$ steps). Finer partitioning lowers
in-sample NLL mechanically, and M2's subset is the better-populated, more
regular steps. Rescored on the common $78{,}076$ steps with two-fold held-out
evaluation: M0 $18.1607$, M1 $16.3760$ (gain $\mathbf{1.7847}$),
M$_{\text{struct}}$ $16.4198$ (gain $1.7409$), M2 $15.3424$
(gain over sequence $\mathbf{1.0336}$). Structure-over-sequence falls
$2.9\times$ and \textbf{the order inverts}: sequence adds more than structure.

What survives is enough. Structure still adds a real $+1.03$ nats \emph{beyond}
sequence, and sequence-alone ($+1.78$) and structure-alone ($+1.74$) are
near-identical and complementary---$3.53$ nats if independent against $2.82$
actual. An encoder reading \textbf{both} is therefore correct, and a
sequence-keyed dinucleotide lookup table still leaves $\sim$$1.03$ nats unused.
Only the comparative superlative is retracted.


\paragraph{The design.} A \textbf{stiffness encoder} consumes the trunk and pair
representations at each step and emits a mean $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^6$ and a
positive-definite precision matrix $F_i \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$, parameterised
through a Cholesky factor so positive-definiteness is structural. It is trained
by the Gaussian negative log-likelihood of the observed deformation:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{stiff}} = \sum_i \left[ \tfrac{1}{2}(x_i - \mu_i)^{\top} F_i (x_i - \mu_i) - \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det F_i \right].
\end{equation}
The $\log\det F$ term is load-bearing: without it the degenerate optimum is
$F \rightarrow 0$---declare everything floppy and pay nothing. With it,
confidence must be earned. At inference $F_i$ \emph{is} the local force-constant
field that $E_{\text{rigid}}$ requires, conditioned on structure rather than
retrieved by sequence.

\paragraph{Acceptance criterion.} The encoder must reach below $14.551$
nats/step on held-out structures. Above $17.579$ it is worse than a lookup table
and the component should be cut.

\section{Training}
\label{sec:training}

NucleicBERT used 192 A100s densely. That is the bar to get under.

\subsection{Optimiser}
\textbf{Muon} orthogonalises gradient momentum through Newton--Schulz iterations
before applying it, reporting roughly $2\times$ compute efficiency against AdamW,
matching quality at about $52\%$ of training FLOPs with $\sim33\%$ memory saving.
It is already validated on a \emph{mixture-of-experts} model at scale (Moonlight,
3B/16B, 5.7T tokens) and is in production use in Kimi K2, so it is proven on the
exact architecture class used here. It applies to 2D matrix parameters---the
expert FFNs and attention projections, i.e.\ most of the model---while
embeddings, norms and scalars remain on AdamW.

\textcolor{risk}{\textbf{Caveat.}} Muon requires fewer optimiser steps but each
step costs more, since Newton--Schulz is a cubic matrix operation. The net win
favours large hidden dimensions and must be benchmarked on our actual parameter
shapes rather than assumed.

\label{sec:loop}

AlphaFold2 recycles three times by default. For PHAROS recycling is

The Hierarchical Reasoning Model literature sharpens this considerably, and the
independent ARC Prize ablation is more informative than the paper itself.

\noindent The loop matters \emph{during training}, not merely at inference. Three
changes follow.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item \textbf{Deep supervision at every segment.} The specification previously
computed loss only after the final recycle. HRM's evidence says supervise each
segment; this costs nothing and has the largest measured effect.
\item \textbf{One-step gradient approximation}---detach hidden states between
segments. This decouples cycle count from activation memory, so \emph{effective
depth becomes free in memory terms}, removing the choice between AlphaFold2's
``final cycle only'' and unaffordable full BPTT.
\item \textbf{Train with more cycles than inference uses} (train 8--16, serve 3).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Deferred: ACT/Q-learning halting, so easy inputs stop early---better
motivated for RNA than for fixed-size puzzle grids, since a clean helix needs one
pass while a four-way junction with a pseudoknot needs many.

\paragraph{Not adopted.} HRM's two-timescale H/L module split, measured at
$\sim$5pp and possibly noise. PHAROS already has a hierarchy---coarse blocks to
fine pairs---justified by measured block occupancy rather than by analogy to
brain oscillations.

\paragraph{Why this is also a data-efficiency argument.} HRM reaches $40.3\%$ on
ARC-AGI with 27M parameters and $\sim$1{,}000 training examples, no pretraining,
on tasks where chain-of-thought models score $0\%$. That is our regime: 6{,}661
unique sequences with 3D structure. It is direct evidence that a small, heavily
recurrent model can beat far larger ones when data is scarce and the task is
structured.

\subsection{Token budget: staged, not slashed}

At 269M active parameters, Chinchilla-optimal is $\sim$5.4B tokens while we plan

$\sim$1.5\% of the full run, converting an unfalsifiable guess about data volume

\label{sec:dyn}

\subsection{One structure is the wrong output for much of RNA}

Riboswitches are \emph{defined} by holding two functional folds, ligand-bound and
unbound, so predicting one is predicting half the biology. The field has moved
off the minimum-free-energy structure: functionally relevant RNAs are
heterogeneous ensembles in which the most stable state may be a minor
subpopulation, and CASP16 now scores alternative conformations as its own track.

\subsection{The harmonic ensemble is nearly free}

Given a predicted structure and a per-step $6\times6$ stiffness field, the
harmonic fluctuation spectrum follows by diagonalising the assembled stiffness---no
sampling, no molecular dynamics. This is the standard elastic-network
construction, already assessed for RNA against MD and SHAPE. PHAROS therefore
emits three outputs alongside coordinates.

(held-out $+1.7847$ and $+1.7409$; the combination adds a further $+1.0336$
beyond sequence). An earlier version claimed structure dominates; that was an
in-sample artefact, corrected in Section~\ref{sec:dyn} (REV-2).

\subsection{What this does not claim}

\begin{quote}
\textcolor{risk}{\textbf{This is an equilibrium fluctuation model.}} It describes
breathing \emph{around} a fold. It is \textbf{not} a folding-pathway predictor.
Cotranscriptional folding is kinetic---structure evolves as the chain elongates
and the system rarely reaches equilibrium, so cotranscriptional products
routinely deviate from thermodynamic predictions. Reconstructing those pathways
requires \emph{time-resolved} probing data, which is in neither our catalogue nor
Ribonanza. Describing a harmonic ensemble as ``predicting how RNA folds'' would
be precisely the class of overclaim that the coevolution retraction in
Section~\ref{sec:coev} was about. The supportable claim is: \emph{predicts the
fold, its local rigidity, its disorder, and the amplitude of motion about it,
under stated ionic conditions.}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Risk this adds (R7).} No settled metric exists for scoring a predicted
RNA ensemble against experiment; RNAnneal compares against just 16
experimentally-resolved riboswitch conformations. Reporting ``3 states'' without
a scoring protocol agreed in advance would be uninterpretable, so single-state
TM-score on blind sets remains the headline and ensemble output is reported
descriptively.

\section{Generalization audit: can this handle \emph{any} RNA?}
\label{sec:gen}

The design claims contact maps, structure and other properties for RNA in
general. This section tests that against the 180 sampled structures rather than
asserting it. Four limits are real; three were undocumented before this audit.

\paragraph{Basis.} Every figure below is re-derived under the single canonical
RNA definition of \S\ref{sec:gen}: a residue is RNA iff its chain declares
\texttt{\_entity\_poly.type = polyribonucleotide} \emph{and} it occupies a
polymer position. \textbf{162 of the 180 sampled structures contain an RNA
polymer entity}, so every structure-count denominator here is $162$, not $180$.
The first pass at this correction updated only four of eleven affected numbers
and left three tables mixing denominators; that inconsistency is defect~\#24.

\subsection{G1 --- single chains fit the context, whole entries do not}

The longest RNA chain has median $86.5$~nt and maximum $3{,}764$~nt, so a
$4{,}096$ context covers every single chain we hold, with $56$ of $162$
structures whose longest chain exceeds $2{,}048$. But \emph{total} RNA per entry
reaches $\mathbf{11{,}478}$ residues, and $44$ of $162$ entries exceed a
$4{,}096$ context. Outside this
sample the gap is larger: lncRNAs ($\sim$19 knt) and viral genomes ($\sim$30 knt)
are an order of magnitude beyond anything considered here.
\textbf{Scope statement required}: PHAROS predicts single RNA chains up to
$4{,}096$~nt; it is not a whole-transcript or whole-ribosome model.

\subsection{G2 --- 99\% of the structural evidence is RNA \emph{in complex}}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{How much of the 3D evidence base is isolated RNA. \meas}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Structures containing protein & $157/162 = \mathbf{96.9\%}$ \\
Median protein chains when present & $\mathbf{11}$ \\
Structures with more than one RNA chain & $118/162 = 72.8\%$ \\
\textbf{RNA residues in protein-containing entries} & $\mathbf{99.70\%}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The architecture takes one RNA sequence and predicts its fold, but essentially
all 3D supervision comes from RNA whose conformation is stabilised by partners
the model never sees. Training on isolated chains asks the model to reproduce,
from sequence alone, a fold that in reality exists only inside a
ribonucleoprotein.

\textcolor{risk}{\textbf{This is the single largest generalization hazard in the
design}}, and it was not acknowledged anywhere before this audit. It is not
fixable by tuning---it is a property of what RNA structural biology has
deposited. The honest options are to restrict claims to plausibly autonomous
folds (riboswitches, aptamers, ribozymes), to supply partner context as an
input, or to report performance split by complexed versus isolated. The last is
mandatory regardless.

\subsection{G3 --- the residue-weighted statistics are ribosome measurements}

Sixty ribosome-like entries ($60/162 = 37.0\%$ of structures) contribute
$286{,}458/306{,}857 = \mathbf{93.35\%}$ of all RNA residues. Every residue-weighted number in this
report---stiffness, ion coordination, block occupancy, the contact-scaling
law---is therefore predominantly a \emph{ribosomal} measurement.

The mitigation is already present, by luck rather than design: the
length-binned tables stratify it. Short chains show block occupancy $16.34\%$
and effective $c$ of $7.9$ against $1.34\%$ and $17.2$ for long chains, so the
$c=20$ budget is generous at \emph{both} ends and the sparse track is safe. But
the headline ``$1.34\%$ occupancy'' is a ribosome figure and must be labelled as
such rather than quoted as ``RNA''.

\subsection{G7 --- modified nucleotides cannot be tokenised}

The vocabulary is five symbols. Measured canonically, $\mathbf{1.04\%}$ of RNA
polymer residues fall outside $\{A,C,G,U\}$---$3{,}189$ instances across
$\mathbf{76}$ distinct modification types, led by pseudouridine ($999$), OMG
($373$), A2M ($365$), OMC and OMU ($268$ each) and inosine ($80$). elDORS is
pre-normalised to five symbols, which is why vocab-5 looked sufficient for
pretraining; structures are not normalised. Seventy-six chemically distinct
modifications observed in 180 structures, led by the most abundant modification
in cellular RNA, is a stronger case for widening the structural vocabulary than
any percentage.

\paragraph{Re-derived in cycle 6 (defects \#22, \#23).} Every analysis script had
rolled its own definition of an RNA residue, and they disagreed on \textbf{72 of
180 structures}. mmCIF declares polymer type, so a residue is RNA iff its chain's
\texttt{\_entity\_poly.type} is \texttt{polyribonucleotide} \emph{and} it occupies
a polymer position. Under that definition G2 \emph{strengthens} ($98.96 \rightarrow
\mathbf{99.70\%}$ of RNA residues in protein-containing entries) and G3 is
unchanged ($93.35\%$), but \textbf{G7's published $8.90\%$ was wrong by
$8.6\times$}: it counted DNA from hybrid duplexes (DT $4{,}608$, DG $4{,}556$,
DA $4{,}440$, DC $4{,}135$) and UNK records ($7{,}036$) that are not in RNA chains
at all---$24{,}775$ of the $27{,}437$ ``modified'' instances.

\paragraph{Defect \#24 --- partial propagation.} The first pass at this
correction updated four of the eleven affected numbers and stopped, and an
unguarded global replace of the canonical values overwrote the \emph{published}
column of the comparison table in the architecture spec, making it read
``$99.70 \rightarrow 99.70$''. Three tables were left mixing the $180$ and $162$
denominators inside a single row-set. It is the third occurrence of the
unguarded-replace failure mode, and the first to corrupt a table whose only
purpose was to record a correction. Every substitution in the repair was made
with an exact-match, count-asserted replace.

\subsection{G6 --- router behaviour at recycle 0 is undefined}

The router is specified to read pairing probability, predicted rigidity and
local density---all produced by the pair track, which runs \emph{after} the
trunk. At recycle 0 none of them exist. $B_{\mathrm{elec}}$ has exactly this
problem and it was resolved explicitly; the router never was. The fix has the
same shape---route on $N_{\text{eff}}/L$ and sequence-local features on the
first pass, switch to structural routing from recycle 1---but it has to be
stated, because an undefined router decides which experts fire.

\subsection{Verdict}

\textbf{Efficiency is the best-verified part of the design.} The hierarchical
pair track runs at $0.96\%$ of dense at $L=4096$ while the dense baseline cannot
run at all; the parameter and FLOP arithmetic reproduces exactly; training is
costed at $\sim$79 A100-hours against NucleicBERT's 192 GPUs.

\textbf{Generalization to ``any RNA'' is not supportable as scoped.} Four
measured limits and one undefined behaviour bound it. None invalidates the
design; together they fix the honest headline: \emph{a single-chain RNA
structure model, up to 4,096 nt, trained predominantly on ribosomal and
complex-embedded RNA, which must report performance split by isolated versus
complexed context.}

\section{Decided parameters and formats}
\label{sec:decide}

\subsection{A conflict the design could not satisfy}

Three requirements were mutually incompatible: the \emph{smallest viable model}
(61M active), the \emph{full 323B-token corpus}, and \emph{4-bit} precision.
\emph{Low-Bit Quantization Favors Undertrained LLMs} (ACL 2025, arXiv
2411.17691, 1500+ checkpoints) finds quantization-induced degradation is worst
for small models trained on many tokens, stating that ``smaller models require
higher precision at high training token counts''.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Where each size sits relative to that law. \meas}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{active} & \textbf{tok/param @323B} & \textbf{$\times$ Chinchilla} & \textbf{4-bit} \\
\midrule
Micro & 12M & $26{,}917$ & $1{,}346\times$ & worst case \\
Mini & 30M & $10{,}767$ & $538\times$ & worst case \\
\textbf{Small} & \textbf{61M} & $\mathbf{5{,}295}$ & $\mathbf{265\times}$ & \textbf{worst case} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

RNA worsens it: measured sequence entropy is $2.0167$ bits/nt against roughly
$11$ for an English token, so the corpus is more redundant than its token count
implies.

\subsection{Decisions, with three reversals}

\textbf{D1} keeps PHAROS-Small. \textbf{D2} (reversal) cuts the token budget
from 323B to a staged 25B, stopping on plateau---the over-training defence was
argued at 269M active, and at 61M it becomes $265\times$ Chinchilla while buying
no information. \textbf{D3} (reversal) trains in BF16, adopts FP8 on Hopper once
the baseline reproduces, and takes \emph{no} 4-bit at this size. \textbf{D4}
defers QAT as a deployment technique. \textbf{D5} routes new attributes in as
input features through one projection rather than a wider $d_{\text{model}}$.
\textbf{D6} adopts two vocabularies. \textbf{D7} (reversal) makes the cost
hardware-explicit.

\paragraph{Why not 4-bit here.} NF4 cannot train from scratch at all---it is
W4A16 with a \emph{frozen} 4-bit base, gradients reaching only LoRA adapters.
NVFP4 \emph{is} a genuine pretraining format, reporting no measurable loss
against an FP8 baseline, but its validation point is 8B parameters and 1T tokens
($125$ tok/param) against our $5{,}295$. MXFP4 needs ${\sim}36\%$ more tokens
than NVFP4 for equal loss.

\paragraph{Defect \#15: the published cost was not a real unit.} A100 (Ampere)
has no FP8 tensor cores; FP8 arrived with Hopper. The cost was quoted in
\textbf{A100-hours} while a $1.60\times$ \textbf{FP8} lever was applied, so
``$\sim$79 A100-hours'' existed on no single machine and ``3.3 days on a single
A100'' was unreachable. Corrected, with levers split by what they depend on
(hardware-independent: Muon $2.00\times$, read down-weighting $1.19\times$,
combined $2.38\times$): at 323B tokens, 126~h on A100 bf16 or 20~h on H100 fp8;
at the decided 25B budget, $\sim$10~h and $\sim$1.6~h respectively.
\textbf{The token cut is a $12.9\times$ saving against FP8's $1.6\times$}, and
unlike a precision lever it costs no accuracy because the dropped tokens are
redundant.

\paragraph{Where the over-provisioned bits go.} A fully attributed nucleotide
carries $58.9$ measured bits; a 512-dim bf16 token holds $8{,}192$---$139\times$
over-provisioned. $d_{\text{model}}$ is \emph{compute} space, not storage, so
attributes are nearly free while width is not: FLOPs go as $6N_{\text{active}}$
and widening $d$ is quadratic in the FFN. Only $26.0$ of those bits are
available at inference; the remainder are supervision targets, and feeding them
in would leak the answer.

\section{Defect \#17: refinement loops were never costed}
\label{sec:loops}

The cost model computed $6 N_{\text{active}} T$ --- the cost of \emph{one} pass
through the blocks. PHAROS-Small runs \textbf{8 refinement loops}, and the
training design specifies deep supervision at \emph{every} segment with a
one-step (detached) gradient, so each segment is its own forward and backward
pass. Compute therefore scales linearly with the loop count. The design even
states that loops ``cost compute but no additional activation memory'', and then
omits the compute. Activation memory indeed does not scale---that is what the
detach buys---but FLOPs do.

understated by exactly its loop count. \meas}

\midrule
\textbf{Small} & 16 & 8 & 128 & \textbf{48} & 61M & \textbf{488M} & \textbf{78} \\

Two headline claims do not survive.

\paragraph{``4.4$\times$ smaller than Base-v2'' collapses to 1.65$\times$.} By
active parameters, 61M against 269M is $4.4\times$. By \emph{effective compute}
(active $\times$ loops) it is 488M against 807M --- only $\mathbf{1.65\times}$.
Eight loops against three consume most of the saving.

\paragraph{The depth comparison mixed training with serving.} Small's 8 is a
\emph{training} loop count (the design says ``train 8--16, serve 3''), while
Base-v2's 3 reads as a serving count---two different quantities in one column.
At a matched serve-3 setting, Small is \textbf{48} effective layers against
Base-v2's \textbf{96}: \textcolor{risk}{\textbf{the claim reverses at
inference}}.

\paragraph{Corrected cost.} The lever chain falls from a claimed $24\times$ to
$\mathbf{5.6\times}$, because the right-sizing step is $1.65\times$ rather than
$4.43\times$ once loops are counted. At the decided 25B-token budget,
PHAROS-Small costs $\mathbf{78}$ A100-hours in bf16 or $\mathbf{12}$ on H100
fp8. Two independently written cost paths agree on both figures.

The token-budget cut ($12.9\times$) therefore remains the largest single lever
in the design---larger than right-sizing and larger than any precision choice.

\paragraph{Two spec gaps closed in cycle 4.} The attention \textbf{head count
was never specified anywhere} ($d=512$, heads unstated); it is now fixed at 8
heads of 64 dims. And ``Motif bank + heads + decoder $\sim$10M'' was a
placeholder whose \textbf{decoder was never specified at all}, so the line could
not be checked. Itemised, the 9 heads cost $1.65$M---the three added for the
dynamics module only $0.80$M between them---the frozen motif bank $0.17$M, and a
plausible 4-block frame-diffusion decoder $\mathbf{12.59}$M, which alone exceeds
the line. Totals rise from 149M/61M to $\sim$153M/$\sim$65M. Heads are cheap;
the decoder is not.

\section{Buildability audit}
\label{sec:build}

Nineteen defects in, the newest failure mode was \emph{components never
specified at all}---the attention head count and the decoder size---then
silently assumed small. This section asks one question of every component:
could a competent engineer implement it from the specification alone, without
inventing a number?

\textbf{Thirty-seven components were enumerated; ten were not specified.} Seven
were genuinely absent (learning rate, batch size, warmup, the five loss weights,
diffusion steps, the MoE bias-update rate, the Gated DeltaNet state size); two
existed only in the reference code or the data catalogue rather than in any
config; one exposed a circularity. All are now closed or marked
\textsc{provisional} with the procedure that fixes them. Invention would have
been worse than deferral: the 5B checkpoint costs about $1.5\%$ of the run, so
each is a cheap sweep rather than a guess.

\subsection{Defect \#20: \texorpdfstring{$B_{\mathrm{motif}}$}{B\_motif} shares the router's circularity}

Three bias terms consume output produced \emph{after} the trunk they feed, not
one. $B_{\mathrm{elec}}$ needs a distance estimate and was given an explicit
first-pass rule. $B_{\mathrm{motif}}$ needs the motif bank's key, which is the
\emph{interaction graph} produced by the pair track---and never had one. The
router's own fix was described but never written into the bias equation. All
three now share a single explicit recycle schedule in the model config.

\subsection{Defect \#21: the MoE copied one DeepSeek ratio and not the other}

The design cites DeepSeekMoE fine-grained segmentation as its template. Checked
against the verified DeepSeek-V3 configuration ($d=7168$, expert
$d_{\text{ff}}=2048$, 1 shared plus 256 routed, 8 activated):

\begin{table}[h]

\midrule
DeepSeek-V3 & $0.286$ & 9 & $\mathbf{2.571\times}$ \\

PHAROS's active FFN capacity is $1.7\times$ below the template it cites and

activation, and the ratio had never been examined. This is a design gap rather
than an error---PHAROS is deliberately data-limited, so less active capacity may
be correct---but it is exactly the kind of parameter that silently caps quality.
The fix is unusually cheap: top-8 of 32 gives $2.50\times$ for $+12.6$M active
parameters and essentially no change in total, because the experts already
exist. The decision is deferred to the 5B checkpoint.

\section{Risks and open questions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item \textbf{Block-detection recall.} Superseded twice, still not eliminated.
The flat top-$K$ design was measured at $\sim$20\% recall and replaced
(Section~\ref{sec:hpt}). Testing then showed the replacement's selectors received
\emph{no gradient at all} and could never have been trained; that is now fixed by
score gating plus a directly supervised block-occupancy loss. The track has a
$100\%$ recall ceiling and the selectors now have labels, but no model has been
trained, so attainment remains unproven.
\item \textbf{Ionic metadata sparsity---audited, partially confirmed}
(Section~\ref{sec:r2}). About $36\%$ of structures yield a recoverable ionic
condition. Worse, recorded Mg\textsuperscript{2+} spans only 5--15 mM because
the PDB is survivorship-biased. The learned ionic response must come from RMDB
titration series, not the PDB. The closed-form physics term and the
Mg\textsuperscript{2+} site head are unaffected.
\item \textbf{Crystallographic ions are not the solution ensemble.} The explicit
head learns site binding, not the diffuse atmosphere. These must not be
conflated.
\item \textbf{B-factor is a noisy rigidity proxy.} Excluding cryo-EM left only
31 of 137 parsed structures usable, which materially shrinks the rigidity label
set.
\item \textbf{Association, not isolated causation.} $\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}$ proximity
and packing density both track folded cores.
\item \textbf{Infrastructure.} NucleicBERT used 192 A100s densely. MoE improves
FLOP efficiency but adds routing and communication complexity.
\item \textbf{Evaluation honesty.} The field ceiling is TM $\approx 0.55$. Any
improvement claim must be made on blind sets---CASP15/16 and RNA-Puzzles---never
on rRNA-saturated random splits. Sequence-identity deduplication is mandatory.
\end{enumerate}

%========================================================
\section{Status}

This is a design specification. No model has been trained and no predictive
performance is claimed. What has been established is the evidentiary basis:
the contact-scaling law, the ion--rigidity coupling, the ion inventory and
coordination chemistry, and the GNRA negative result, all measured on 180
non-redundant structures with reproducible scripts. The next phase is to
implement the sparse pair track and validate proposal recall before any
large-scale training is attempted.
