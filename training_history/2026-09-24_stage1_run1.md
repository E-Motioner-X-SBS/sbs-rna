# Run 1 — stage 1 (MLM), 24–25 September 2026

`shared400`, 394.7M total / 325.7M active. Resumed at step 3,000 / 0.395B
tokens, stopped by hand at step 10,800 / 1.574B. Muon at lr 0.02 on 222
matrices (343M parameters), AdamW at 6e-4 on the rest, `--n-loops 2` fixed,
4B-token cosine.

Raw log: [`logs/2026-09-24_stage1_run1.log`](logs/2026-09-24_stage1_run1.log).

## What the numbers did

**The training loss is not a progress signal and never was.** Over 78 logged
steps it swung between **0.470 and 1.876 bits** — a 3.6× range — and the swing
is the shard, not the model. Any two consecutive step lines can differ by more
than the entire run's improvement. Reading them as progress is the single
easiest mistake this log invites.

**The held-out curve tracked overnight — 26 readings, 1.9800 → 1.9581 bits,
best 1.6045 at step 8,000 — is not a measurement of the model.** Its sample is
`eldors_c020_shard0004` alone, and **52.7% of it is 20–79 nt against the
corpus's 6.5%**. Fitted, it has a slope of −0.025 bits per 1,000 steps and a
residual scatter of **0.106 bits** — four times the slope per 1,000 steps. The
curve is mostly noise around a weak trend, and the noise has structure: it
tracks which length band the training pool is currently serving.

Scored per band on a corpus-spanning sample, steps 9,000 → 9,500:

| band | 9000 | 9250 | 9500 | swing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20–79 | 1.6271 | 1.8394 | 1.9775 | **+0.3504** |
| 80–159 | 1.8632 | 1.8964 | 1.8989 | +0.0357 |
| 160–319 | 1.9021 | 1.8963 | 1.9008 | **−0.0013** |
| 320–639 | 1.7737 | 1.8202 | 1.9225 | +0.1488 |
| 640–1024 | 1.9278 | 1.9085 | 1.9209 | **−0.0069** |

The +0.303-bit "collapse" that looked like the run falling apart is **+0.074
corpus-weighted**, and two of five bands do not move at all.

**Where the model actually got to.** Corpus-weighted, against the corpus's own
2.0165 bits/nt unigram entropy:

| step | tracked (c020) | corpus-weighted | better than unigram |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9,000 | 1.6495 | **1.8396** | 18.2% → **8.8%** |
| 9,500 | 1.9527 | 1.9135 | 3.2% → 5.1% |
| 9,750 (stratified) | 1.9581 | **1.9221** | — → **4.7%** |

Roughly **5–9% better than base frequencies at 1.5B tokens**. Every "18–20%"
figure reported during the run came from the short-sequence sample and is about
twice the true value.

**What the model did learn, unambiguously.** The Watson-Crick gap — accuracy
with the pairing partner visible minus accuracy with it masked, two conditions
differing only in whether the partner is readable — was **positive in all 19
readings**, mean **+0.0926**, 3–4σ. That is pairing being used, not base
composition. But its trend is **flat**: −0.0008 per 1,000 steps at −0.28σ over
4,750 steps. The pairing signal was already present at step 5,000 and did not
grow. Those are two different curves and the bits curve should not be read as
evidence about this one.

**The MoE did not collapse.** Balance ranged 0.177–0.225 with a mean of 0.1952
and per-1,000-step means of 0.183, 0.199, 0.202, 0.200, 0.195, 0.203 — flat.
Audited directly at step 7,000: mean routing width **15.28** of a possible 512,
widest token 330, router entropy 7.255 of 9.000 bits, **0.0% dead experts**,
**288.5 effective experts of 512**. Nucleus routing does what §5.3 claims. Note
the logged balance is a SUM over 18 blocks, so 0.197 is **1.09 per block** — 9%
above uniform, not the 20× concentration the raw figure suggests.

## What was wrong with the run, as opposed to the model

- **The token budget was 0.37 of one epoch.** The corpus is 40.3M sequences and
  **10.94B usable tokens**; 4B never saw it once, and 325.7M active parameters
  want ~6.5B by Chinchilla.
- **A training pool was 0.54 of one length-sorted shard** — total-variation
  distance 0.342 from the corpus, pool mean 201 nt against 320. The reader
  shuffled shard *order*, which changes which band a pool gets but cannot mix
  them. So the run saw a narrow length distribution for 131k sequences, then a
  discontinuous jump.
- **`--max-batch 512` bound on the sequence count, not the token budget**:
  92.5% of batches capped on short shards, real tokens per step 83,899 against
  194,607 on long ones — a **2.5× swing in effective batch size** driven by
  nothing but which shard was streaming.
- **The model was usable at exactly one recycle depth.** 1.6632 bits at the 2
  loops it trained on, 1.9718 at 1, 1.9711 at 4, 2.0064 at 8 — retaining 2.9%
  of its advantage at the 8 the config advertised.
- MFU sat at **13.9%**.

## Was it worth keeping the weights?

Yes. Nothing wrong was in the weights — the defects were in the data order, the
batch shape, the budget and the measurement. Run 2 resumes from step 10,750
(1.569B tokens) rather than starting over, and the checkpoint is preserved as
`pretrain_shared400.pre_restart_1.569B.pt`.

## What is still not known

The endpoint is **not predictable from this curve**. A power law with a floor
and a log-linear fit describe the 24 clean readings about equally well (rms
0.0536 vs 0.0579) and disagree at 4B tokens by **0.200 bits** — more than half
the total improvement measured. The binding constraint is scatter, not token
range, which is why the pool and batch fixes matter beyond training throughput.
