### The OOM cascade, and why it cost more than one batch

The auto-tune grew while `peak < 0.85 × want`, a memory criterion, and
extrapolated memory **linearly** in the token budget (`budget × want/peak`).
Memory is not linear — attention is B·L² and the pool composition moves
underneath. Peak was 58.8 GiB against a 60.6 threshold, so it grew 10%, and
peak went past 79.

Four OOMs followed at the same step, each cutting 15%, ending at **52% of the
pre-OOM budget and 90% of where the run started**. Then `n_oom == 0` in the
growth condition made that permanent: the remaining 6.2B tokens would have
trained at half the batch, with nothing re-testing whether they had to. Batch
size is the quantity that correlates with held-out bits here (partial r −0.52),
so it was not a free safety margin.

Fixed two ways. In code, the level that OOMed is remembered and the budget may
climb back toward 90% of it after 1,000 clean steps — simulated on the real
sequence it recovers 117,760 → 204,800. In the config, the budget is now
**fixed at 180,224** with `--vram-target 0.65`, which puts the estimated peak
above the growth threshold so the auto-tune never fires at all. Removing the
failure mode beat relying on recovery from it.
